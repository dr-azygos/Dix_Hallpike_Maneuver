      Mounting

      Headset (recommended): put the phone in a VR-style headset (Cardboard-type) with the screen facing the eyes. The front camera films the eyes, and the uniform screen light acts like Frenzel goggles by removing the fixation target. You follow voice prompts and beeps.

      Forehead: strap the phone to the forehead, screen facing out, so you can read the angle display. There is no eye video in this mode.

      Calibration

      Sit the patient on the couch facing its foot end, head straight, looking ahead. The app calibrates after 2–3 seconds of stillness. All target angles are measured from this pose, so the patient must lie back along the couch.

      Dix-Hallpike

      1. Turn the head 45° to the test side.
      2. Lie the patient back briskly with the head hanging about 20° below the couch. Recording starts as they go down; the time to reach position is noted.
      3. Observe (default 45 s), then sit up and watch for reversal.
      4. Review the video at slow speed. Tap Onset and End to measure latency and duration, choose the pattern, and the app suggests the side to treat.

      Epley

      Each position is reached when the head is within the angle tolerance. The hold timer only runs while the head stays there. Cues give the correction still needed, relative to the patient’s body: rotate left/right, chin up/down, tilt toward a shoulder.

      Reading the video

      The front camera shows the patient as you would see them facing them: the patient’s right eye is on the left of the image. Use the rotate button if the phone was mounted in landscape. Posterior canal BPPV gives upbeating nystagmus with the upper pole of the eye beating toward the lower (affected) ear.

      Accuracy

      Angles come from the phone’s gyroscope-based orientation fusion. Expect a few degrees of error, plus slow drift of rotation about the vertical over several minutes. Recalibrate if a session runs long.
